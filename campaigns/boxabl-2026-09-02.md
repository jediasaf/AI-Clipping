## 3. Question 1 — the realization discrepancy

Scout's arithmetic: $13,100 ÷ 18.7M = $0.70/1k effective, above the $0.50 headline, framed as
possible upside.

**Finding: the premise is faulty, and the true direction is almost certainly the opposite of
what Scout inferred.**

1. **The numerator is not creator earnings.** The page shows budget *used*, and displays no
   payout figure (VERIFIED). Scout converted "used" into "paid". Budget consumption includes
   platform economics the creator never sees.
2. **Fees are charged on top of the brand's budget (VERIFIED, contentrewards.com/articles/v2-is-here):**
   "A 2.7% plus $0.37 Whop processing fee is now added on top of your budget," and "The Content
   Rewards platform fee is unchanged: 10%, or 8% for verified accounts."
3. **Arithmetic check (INFERENCE):** 18.7M × $0.50/1k = $9,350 of view-earned value against
   $13.1K of budget consumed — a ratio of ~1.40. Brand-side platform + processing fees (~13%)
   explain part of that gap but not all of it.
4. **Residual candidates, none confirmable from outside:** a higher CPM earlier in the campaign
   blended down to $0.50; affiliate-program payouts drawn from the same pool (the brief documents
   form fills at $1 each, sales at 3% of unit price, and recruit bonuses at 0.5% / $0.25 — Scout
   did not report this program at all, and it is a strong candidate for non-view spend inside the
   same budget); leaderboard/bonus prizes; view-counter lag.

**Answer: it cannot be determined from outside, and it does not matter, because $0.70 is not
bankable under any explanation.** The contracted rate is $0.50/1k gross. The excess is explained
by mechanisms we neither control nor receive.

**What an editing hour is actually worth (VERIFIED):** contentrewards.com/creators states the
creator-side CPM fee is "25% to start, then 20% past $1,000 lifetime, 12.5% past $2,500, 7% past
$5,000." At N=0 lifetime earnings we are in the 25% tier.

> **Net rate to us: $0.375 per 1,000 views, not $0.50 — and only for views accruing within
> 7 days of approval.**

That is a 25% haircut on Scout's headline, on top of a hard 7-day earning window. Break-even for
a single $1 payout is ~2,667 views. $50 of revenue requires ~133,000 qualifying views inside
seven days. This is the correction with the largest economic consequence in the audit.

## 5. Question 3 — rejection risk from the soft rules

Scout is right to be worried, and the risk is real but bounded.

**"Positive vibes. No mocking, no negativity toward Boxabl"** — subjective, but the failure mode
is predictable. Boxabl is a company with a contested public narrative (delivery timelines,
production volume). Any clip that even neutrally engages with skepticism is exposed. The safe
line is *product-forward wonder*: the home itself, the fold-out mechanism, the factory scale.
Avoid: comparisons, price-per-square-foot analysis, "is it worth it", delivery/timeline talk,
anything with an implied verdict.

**The stock/ticker prohibition** is actually the *lower* risk of the two — it is mechanical, not
subjective. "DO NOT MENTION BOXABL STOCK, OR THER TRADING TICKER IN ANY CAPTIONS" [sic]. It is
easy to comply with absolutely: no ticker, no "invest", no "shares", no "IPO", no valuation talk,
in caption or on-screen text. Note the rule as written says "IN ANY CAPTIONS" — whether it also
binds *spoken audio in the source footage* is **UNKNOWN — NEEDS VERIFICATION**, and this matters
because Graham Stephan's and the NYSE interview footage are finance content likely to discuss
exactly this. Clipping the NYSE interview or Graham Stephan without hitting stock talk is
genuinely hard.

**Appeal exposure:** no appeals process is documented anywhere (UNKNOWN — NEEDS VERIFICATION).
A rejection is therefore presumptively final and the editing hours are presumptively lost.
That is the real risk — not the rejection rate, but the absence of recourse.

**What a clip must do to be safely inside the line:** Boxabl-owned footage only; whole home
visible; product-forward positive framing; no finance/stock/valuation content in caption, overlay,
or audible source audio; @boxabl in caption (URL on X); "BOXABL" spelled correctly in every
burned-in caption; real editing with hook, cuts and overlays; no other brand watermarks; ≥ a few
thousand views needed to be worth reviewing at all.

## 6. Copyright / authorization chain — the central defect

**Finding: the authorization Boxabl grants is not established to be authorization Boxabl holds.**

- Copyright in an audiovisual work vests in its author/producer. For the eight third-party
  videos, that is Enes Yilmazer, Orkun Isitmak, FaZe Rug, Graham Stephan, Airrack, 808, Ellie in
  Space and Irina Dee respectively — **not** Boxabl, which is the *subject* filmed, not the author.
  (INFERENCE from ordinary authorship principles — but a strong one, and the burden is on the
  campaign to rebut it, not on us to assume it away.)
- **Evidence pointing against Boxabl ownership (VERIFIED):** Boxabl's own Facebook post about the
  Enes Yilmazer video thanks him for "stopping by Factory-1" and directs viewers to "watch the
  full video" on his channel. Boxabl publicly frames itself as host and the creator as owner.
- **No licence or sublicence is evidenced anywhere.** The content doc asserts approval in a single
  unsigned line; it names no licensor, no scope, no term, and no grant of sublicensing rights.
  Whether Boxabl holds sublicensable rights in these videos: **UNKNOWN — NEEDS VERIFICATION.**
- **The brief contradicts the content doc** by calling the source "Boxabl's own footage" when it
  is majority third-party. A brief that mischaracterises its own footage provenance is not a
  reliable basis for a rights representation.
- **The download instruction is independently non-compliant (VERIFIED):** the content doc directs
  clippers to a third-party YouTube-to-MP4 converter. Downloading YouTube content other than
  through features YouTube provides is contrary to YouTube's Terms of Service, irrespective of who
  owns the copyright. The campaign is instructing an act that breaches the platform's terms.
- **The unattributed "Elon Musk clip"** on Google Drive has no identifiable provenance and carries
  both copyright and likeness exposure. **UNKNOWN — NEEDS VERIFICATION.** Do not touch it.

CLAUDE.md lists "use unauthorized copyrighted material" as a hard boundary. On the third-party
footage, authorization is asserted but not established, by a party with visible reason not to hold
it. **That subset is RED and is not rescued by owner override of the campaign as a whole** — an
override can accept commercial risk, but the missing item here is a third party's rights, which is
not the owner's to waive.

The Boxabl-owned-channel subset does not have this problem. That is the compliant path.

## 10. Risk rating: YELLOW

Not GREEN: the copyright chain for the majority of the approved footage is unestablished; the
net rate is 25% below the headline; the earning window is 7 days, not indefinite; and five
material rule/figure items in Scout's report were wrong or missing. Several checklist items
remain UNKNOWN.

Not RED: the campaign is real, funded, free to join, has ~$71.9K remaining, pays through a
verifiable mechanism, and — critically — **has a genuinely compliant path** using Boxabl's own
channel footage. Rating the whole campaign RED would discard that path and would be rounding down
to feel safe.

**Top three risks**
1. **Rights defect on third-party footage.** Boxabl's "approved" line is not evidenced authority
   over eight third-party creators' videos, and Boxabl's own public posts frame those creators as
   the owners. Hard boundary.
2. **Economics are ~25% worse than presented, with a 7-day cliff.** $0.375/1k net, views only
   count for 7 days from approval, and clips under ~2,000 views may not be reviewable at all.
3. **Unappealable subjective rejection.** "Positive vibes" plus the stock/ticker ban are enforced
   by an undocumented review queue with no documented appeals route, so a rejection destroys the
   editing hours with no recourse.

## 11. What would make this GREEN

1. Written confirmation from the operator that Boxabl holds sublicensable rights in the
   third-party videos — naming the licensor and scope. *Or*, absent that, permanently restricting
   sourcing to the Boxabl-owned channel, which removes the defect without needing the answer.
2. Owner joins the campaign (free) and the post-join terms are read, resolving: follower/account-age
   gates, one-account-per-person, qualifying-view definition, bot filtering, per-account and
   per-period caps, submission and approval SLA, and whether any appeals route exists.
3. Written clarification of whether the stock/ticker ban extends to audible source audio, not just
   captions.
4. Stated minimum and maximum clip duration.
5. Confirmation of the watermark position: is a Boxabl logo required, permitted, or neither, and
   are platform-origin watermarks disqualifying.
6. The parallel YouTube reused-content audit returns before any Shorts upload.

## 12. Conditions for proceeding under YELLOW

Binding if the owner elects to proceed:

1. **Boxabl-owned-channel footage only.** No Enes Yilmazer, Orkun Isitmak, FaZe Rug, Graham
   Stephan, Airrack, 808, Ellie in Space, Irina Dee, or the unattributed Elon Musk clip. This
   condition is not subject to commercial override.
2. **Join before editing.** Zero editing hours until post-join terms are read and §11(2) resolved.
3. **Cap initial exposure** to a small number of test clips until one submission has actually been
   accepted and paid. Do not scale on an unvalidated payout path.
4. **Model at $0.375/1k with a 7-day window**, never $0.50 and never $0.70.
5. **No YouTube Shorts upload** until the parallel reused-content audit reports.
6. **Do not use the third-party MP4 converter** named in the content doc.
7. KYC and identity-document submission is the owner's decision alone.

Any relaxation of condition 1 requires an explicit owner override recorded in
`experiments/experiment-log.md`, and my position is that condition 1 should not be overridden,
because the right being waived is not ours to waive.
