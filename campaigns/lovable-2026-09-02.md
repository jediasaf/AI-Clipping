## 4. Source material and rights — THE BLOCKING DEFECT

### What the source material actually is

The brief provides **22 YouTube links** and says *"Must only use podcasts provided."* Every one
is a **third-party podcast, conference talk, or interview show at which Anton Osika was a guest**:

Shira Lazar · **Sequoia Capital** · **20VC with Harry Stebbings** (×2) · **Slush 2025** ·
**Accel** · **Stripe** · SmoothViews · TechBBQ · **Pioneers of AI / Masters of Scale** ·
**This Week in Startups** (×2) · **Lenny's Podcast** · Matthieu Stefani – Génération Do It
Yourself · RAISE Summit · Silicon Valhalla · Newcomer · Audio Tokens · Brett Malinowski ·
AIAW Podcast · Antler Global · roompass.

**Not one of the 22 is a Lovable-owned channel.** I checked the full list. Zero.

Anton Osika is VERIFIED as co-founder and CEO of Lovable (multiple independent sources including
TechCrunch and Wikipedia). But **being the interviewee in a podcast does not give you or your
company the copyright in the podcast recording.** The audiovisual work belongs to the publisher —
Sequoia, Stripe, Accel, 20VC, Lenny Rachitsky, Slush, and so on.

### FLAG — this is the Boxabl defect, and here the contract makes it worse

The Boxabl audit found a brand's blanket "approved" line that did not evidence authority over
third-party creators' videos. **The same pattern is present here, in a more advanced form.**

- **[CR-t §19.1]** grants a licence only *"Where a Brand provides footage or assets"*. Lovable
  provides **links to other people's YouTube videos**, not footage or assets. And a brand cannot
  grant a licence to a work it does not own. **The §19.1 grant is empty as to all 22 sources.**
- **[CR-t §19.2]** then assigns the clearance burden to us, explicitly:
  *"You warrant that your Clip does not infringe anyone's rights, and that you have cleared all
  third-party rights it uses, including music and any other third-party content."*
- **I searched §19 and the surrounding terms specifically for a brand warranty of ownership, a
  creator indemnity, or any allocation of liability for a copyright claim or takedown. There is
  none.** The terms are silent on what happens if the brand does not in fact own the footage.

So the contract structure is: **the brand warrants nothing about the footage, and we warrant
everything.** We would be signing a representation that we have cleared rights we have not
cleared and cannot clear, over 22 videos owned by well-resourced counterparties including three
major venture firms and Stripe.

**There is a second, independent defect.** The brief supplies no downloadable assets — only
YouTube watch URLs. To produce a clip we must **download from YouTube**, which YouTube's Terms of
Service prohibit absent permission from the rights holder. Neither Lovable nor MediaMaxxing
evidences any authority over those channels.

**This engages CLAUDE.md's never-do-automatically list directly: "use unauthorized copyrighted
material."** Per the Boxabl precedent, a rights defect of this kind is **not curable by owner
override** — an override can accept commercial risk, but it cannot manufacture a licence from a
party that never granted one.

**Status: VERIFIED DEFECT.** Not an unknown. I read the licence clause and I read the source list;
they do not meet.

## 5. Economics, budget, burn and runway — Scout's central claim does not survive contact

Scout's case is that this is *"the only candidate where a single breakout clip is not capped at
$250."* The **$1,000 per-clip cap is VERIFIED** (§2 item 13). But three verified facts, none of
which was in Scout's report, drain the asymmetry out of it.

### 5a. The 7-day earning window is the finding that changes the case

> "On CPM your clip earns for 7 days from approval, then the payout is held 3 days" — **[CR-c]**

At $1/1,000 views, **reaching the $1,000 cap requires 1,000,000 qualifying views within 7 days of
approval.** Not lifetime views. Seven days.

Set that against the campaign's own observed performance **[C]**: across **109 creators over
~30 days**, the **top earner has 18.4K views**. Second 13.2K. Third 7,927.

**The cap is ~54× beyond the best result any of 109 creators has achieved.** It is real, it is
not a trap, and it is also **effectively unreachable on current evidence**. A $1,000 ceiling that
no participant has come within 2% of is not a live asymmetry; it is a number on a page.

Scout reads the low top-earner figure as *"an unsolved creative problem rather than saturation."*
That reading is defensible and I am not dismissing it. But note that it is **an untested
hypothesis about our own superiority over 109 other participants**, and it is the single
assumption the entire economic case rests on. It should be labelled **EXPERIMENT**, not treated
as an established opportunity.

### 5b. Net rate, corrected

$1.00/1k gross → **25% Content Rewards CPM creator fee at our tier (lifetime earnings $0)** →
**$0.75/1k net.** Scout's $0.75 figure is **VERIFIED**, and the fee genuinely steps down
(20% past $1,000 lifetime, 12.5% past $2,500, 7% past $5,000) — so this improves with volume.

**UNKNOWN — NEEDS VERIFICATION: whether the $1,000 cap and the $0.99 minimum are gross or net.**
Neither **[C]** nor **[CR-c]** says. If gross, a capped clip nets $750.

Realistic per-clip revenue at observed performance: an 18.4K-view clip earns **$18.40 gross /
$13.80 net**. That is the actual unit economics on this campaign today.

### 5c. Budget, burn, runway, end date

- **Budget:** $50K, **$4.9K consumed, ~$45.1K remaining** — VERIFIED as displayed **[C]**.
- **End date:** **none exists.** VERIFIED-ABSENT on **[C]**, and **[CR-t §6.4]** confirms budget
  exhaustion is the terminator: *"remaining budget stays available for new Submissions until it
  is exhausted."*
  **This is genuinely good** — it removes the "End Date reached" termination risk flagged in
  `verification-2026-09-02.md`. Budget exhaustion is the only clock.
- **Runway ≈ 9 months:** **INFERENCE**, not verified. It is $45.1K ÷ $4.9K-per-month, which
  assumes flat burn. Burn is not flat in a campaign whose participant count is growing, and 109
  creators are already enrolled. Treat 9 months as an optimistic ceiling.
- **The $50K is not committed.** *"If we get good results, budget will continue to be re-topped"*
  **[B]** is a statement of intent about *adding* money, which implies the current pool is what
  it is and nothing is guaranteed beyond it. **No party has committed to maintaining the budget.**

### 5d. An arithmetic discrepancy worth noting, not over-reading

6.6M views × $1/1,000 = **$6,600** of implied gross earnings, against **$4.9K** reported consumed —
a ~26% gap. Two candidate explanations: (i) the budget figure is net of the 25% creator fee, or
(ii) roughly a quarter of submitted views were rejected or never qualified. Explanation (ii)
would imply a meaningful rejection rate.

**I cannot distinguish between them and I am not going to pick one.** The view total is also
labelled for a specific window (Aug 3 – Sep 2) while the spend figure may be lifetime, which
could explain the gap entirely. **UNKNOWN — NEEDS VERIFICATION.** Recorded because if it is (ii),
it is the only rejection-rate signal available on this campaign.

## 6. Subjective and self-contradictory rules — unappealable rejection risk

### 6a. "Majority tier 1 viewership" — the Bible BFF pattern, present here

This is the worst rule in the brief and it is the one that most resembles the undisclosed
geography gate that Bible BFF hid.

- **"Tier 1" is never defined.** No country list. Common usage varies (US/UK/CA/AU vs. that plus
  Western Europe vs. broader) and the brief picks none.
- **"Majority" has no threshold.** 50%? Of views? Of followers?
- **No measurement method is stated** — whose analytics, over what window.
- **No consequence is stated.** It is not phrased as a rejection ground, but it also is not
  phrased as a preference. It is a fragment.
- **We cannot pre-verify compliance.** Audience geography is an *output* of the algorithm, not an
  input we control. We would produce and post before we could know whether we passed.

Combined with **"nothing approves automatically"** and rejection grounds that are illustrative
rather than exhaustive (*"Usually the brief: …"*), this is a rule that **can be applied against us
after the work is done, on a standard we were never told, with one appeal and a ~10-business-day
response.** That is the definition of unappealable rejection risk.

### 6b. The 10-minute submission window

*"Submit videos within 10 minutes of posting"* **[B]** is stricter than the platform's own
30-minute rule **[CR-c]**. The stricter rule governs. This is not unreasonable but it is
**operationally binding**: it requires a human present at the keyboard within 10 minutes of every
publish. Since publishing is human-approved anyway under CLAUDE.md, this is compatible — but it
must be designed into the posting run, not discovered on the day.

### 6c. "Must show Anton and/or Lovable" vs. retention editing

*"Can't clip parts of the podcasts without it being about lovable"* constrains clip selection to
Lovable-relevant segments. Reasonable and clear as a rule. Worth flagging to `clip-miner` only as
a scoping constraint: the highest-retention moment in a founder podcast is frequently a personal
or general-startup anecdote, which this rule excludes.

### 6d. `#LovablePartner` as a disclosure

The required tag asserts a partnership. Two issues: (i) it is unverified that Lovable authorized
the campaign (§1); (ii) whether `#LovablePartner` alone constitutes adequate paid-partnership
disclosure under advertising rules is **UNKNOWN — NEEDS VERIFICATION**, and note that
*"missing tags or disclosure"* is a named rejection ground **[CR-c]** — implying disclosure and
tags may be treated as separate obligations.

## 8. Risk rating

# RED

**Do not produce clips.**

The rating is driven by **one defect, not by the accumulated unknowns.**

**The binding reason (§4):** the campaign directs us to clip 22 YouTube videos owned by third
parties — Sequoia, Stripe, Accel, 20VC, Lenny's Podcast, Slush and others — none owned by the
brand. The platform's licence clause grants rights only over footage *"a Brand provides"*, which
cannot reach works the brand does not own, and the very next clause requires us to **warrant that
we have cleared all third-party rights**. There is **no brand warranty of ownership and no
indemnity** anywhere in the terms. Producing these clips means using copyrighted material without
evidenced authorization — explicitly on CLAUDE.md's never-do-automatically list — while
contractually representing the opposite.

**Per the Boxabl precedent, this specific defect is not curable by owner override.** An override
can accept commercial risk; it cannot create a licence the rights holders never granted. If the
owner wishes to proceed anyway, that is their call to make, but it should be recorded in
`experiments/experiment-log.md` as an accepted *legal* exposure, not as a normal risk override,
and it does not become compliant by being approved.

**Aggravating factors, each sufficient for YELLOW on its own:**

- *"Majority tier 1 viewership"* — an undefined, unmeasurable audience-geography gate we cannot
  pre-verify, in a system where **"nothing approves automatically"** and rejection grounds are
  illustrative rather than closed (§6a).
- The Whop rejection protections we believed we had **do not apply** — `contentrewards.com` is a
  different operator (§1).
- The **$1,000 cap is real but requires 1M views in 7 days**, against a field best of 18.4K
  across 109 creators over 30 days (§5a). The asymmetry that justifies the campaign's ranking is
  ~54× outside all observed evidence.
- **Nine checklist items are simply not written down**: clip duration min/max, editing
  requirements, prohibited modifications, watermark rules, required captions, multi-account
  policy, bot-filter methodology, gross-vs-net on the cap, and whether YouTube means Shorts.
- **Unverified that Lovable authorized the campaign**, while the brief requires us to publicly
  tag `#LovablePartner`.

**What is genuinely good here, and should not be lost:** no entry gates that fail us at N=0, no
application step, no participation cost, no end date, withdrawals with no minimum and no fee, a
fee that steps down with volume, a verified per-clip cap far above the board, and an appeal path.
**If the rights defect were cured, this would be a strong YELLOW moving toward GREEN.** The
campaign's problem is its source list, not its terms.
